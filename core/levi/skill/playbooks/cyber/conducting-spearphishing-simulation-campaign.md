## Purpose
Phishing simulations measure and improve human resilience -- when run ethically and tied to training. This playbook builds a simulation program: stakeholder approval, realistic but fair templates, careful targeting, immediate teachable moments, and metrics that show real improvement over time.

## When to use
- Establishing or maturing a security-awareness measurement program.
- Baseling click and report rates before a training investment.
- Testing resilience against current real-world phishing trends.
- Meeting compliance requirements for awareness testing.

## Prerequisites
- Leadership, legal, and HR approval for the program.
- Simulation platform with template library and reporting.
- Defined target groups and exclusion handling (for example, employees on leave).
- Training content ready for users who click.

## Procedure
1. Get program approval. Document objectives, template boundaries, data handling, and how results will and will not be used; get sign-off.
2. Establish the baseline. Run an initial campaign across the organization to measure click, credential-submit, and report rates.
3. Design fair templates. Base lures on real current threats; avoid templates that exploit personal trauma, major events, or HR actions like layoffs.
4. Target thoughtfully. Vary difficulty by role risk; include executives and IT, not just general staff.
5. Deliver teachable moments. Show immediate, constructive feedback to users who click; offer short training, not punishment.
6. Measure reporting, not just clicking. Track the report rate as the key metric; a culture that reports is more valuable than one that merely avoids clicks.
7. Trend over time. Run campaigns regularly; report click-rate and report-rate trends to leadership, segmented by department.
8. Evolve with the threat landscape. Update templates as real attacker tactics change; retire templates everyone recognizes.

## Expected outputs
- Approved simulation program with baseline metrics.
- Campaign reports showing click, submit, and report rates by group.
- Trend data demonstrating program effectiveness.

## Pitfalls
- Using results punitively destroys trust and suppresses real phishing reports.
- Cruel or traumatic lures cause genuine harm and HR incidents.
- One-off simulations without follow-up training change nothing.
- Measuring only clicks ignores the reporting behavior that actually stops campaigns.
