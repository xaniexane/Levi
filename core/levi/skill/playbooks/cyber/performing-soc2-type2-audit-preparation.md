## Purpose
- Enter the SOC 2 Type 2 observation period with controls designed, implemented, and evidenced.
- Avoid the common failure of controls that exist on paper but lack operating-effectiveness evidence.
- Build an evidence collection rhythm the team can sustain for the full audit period.

## When to use
- Six to nine months before the intended Type 2 observation period starts.
- When customers begin requiring SOC 2 reports in procurement.
- After a Type 1 report, to prepare for the Type 2 examination.
- When remediating findings from a previous audit period.

## Prerequisites
- Defined trust services criteria in scope and system boundaries documented.
- An engaged auditor or readiness assessor familiar with SOC 2.
- Control owners assigned across security, engineering, HR, and operations.
- A GRC tool or structured evidence repository.

## Procedure
1. Define scope: system boundaries, in-scope criteria, and the observation period dates.
2. Perform a readiness assessment mapping current controls to each criterion, rating design and likely operating effectiveness.
3. Remediate design gaps first: missing policies, undefined processes, and unassigned ownership.
4. Implement evidence collection: configure the recurring artifacts each control needs (tickets, logs, review records).
5. Run the controls through a full cycle before the observation period to shake out process failures.
6. Train control owners on what auditors will ask for and what good evidence looks like.
7. Conduct internal testing mid-period: sample evidence the way the auditor will and fix deficiencies.
8. Manage exceptions formally: document, remediate, and retest any control failures during the period.
9. Prepare the system description: accurate, complete, and consistent with actual operations.
10. Coordinate with the auditor on evidence requests, walkthroughs, and sampling methodology.
11. Track all auditor requests in one place with owners and due dates to avoid last-minute scrambles.
12. After the report, convert any qualifications or observations into next period's remediation plan.

## Expected outputs
- A readiness assessment with remediated design gaps.
- Sustained evidence collection covering the full observation period.
- A clean audit with managed exceptions and a next-period improvement plan.
- A control rationalization review removing redundant or low-value controls.
- A customer-facing FAQ explaining the SOC 2 report and its scope.

## Pitfalls
- Starting evidence collection after the observation period begins; gaps in the early months cannot be recreated.
- Writing policies nobody follows; auditors test operating effectiveness, not document quality.
- Underestimating the effort; assign real owner time, not spare-time attention.
- Scope creep mid-period; freeze boundaries early and change-control any expansion.
