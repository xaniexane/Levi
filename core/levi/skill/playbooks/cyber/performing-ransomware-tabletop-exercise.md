## Purpose
- Test the ransomware response plan with the people who will execute it, before a real incident.
- Surface gaps in decision authority, communications, and recovery capabilities in a safe setting.
- Build muscle memory for executives and technical teams on ransom decisions and public communications.

## When to use
- At least annually, and after significant changes to infrastructure, backups, or leadership.
- When onboarding new executives or incident response team members.
- After real incidents elsewhere in the sector, to check readiness against current tactics.
- When insurers or regulators ask for evidence of preparedness.

## Prerequisites
- A facilitator who understands ransomware scenarios and can keep discussion honest.
- The current incident response plan, backup and recovery procedures, and contact lists.
- Participants from IT, security, legal, communications, and executive leadership.
- A scenario with timed injects that escalate realistically.

## Procedure
1. Define exercise objectives: what decisions and processes are being tested.
2. Build a realistic scenario with injects: initial detection, spread, ransom note, data-leak threat, and media inquiry.
3. Brief participants on ground rules: this is a safe space, rank does not matter, and the goal is finding gaps.
4. Walk through detection and declaration: who decides this is ransomware, and how is the team activated.
5. Discuss containment decisions: what gets isolated, who has authority, and what breaks when systems go offline.
6. Work through the ransom decision: legal constraints, insurer involvement, and how payment would even be executed.
7. Exercise communications: internal updates, customer notifications, regulator reporting, and press handling.
8. Walk through recovery: backup restoration order, integrity verification, and criteria for returning to production.
9. Capture every gap, confusion, and missing contact as a formal finding during the exercise.
10. Assign owners and deadlines for each finding and track them like incident action items.
11. Update the response plan with what was learned and schedule the next exercise.

## Expected outputs
- A findings list with owners and deadlines covering decision, communication, and recovery gaps.
- An updated ransomware response plan.
- Participants who have practiced the hardest decisions before they count.
- A participant confidence survey before and after, showing preparedness improvement.
- Updated call trees and contact lists validated during the exercise.

## Pitfalls
- Running the exercise with only technical staff; the ransom and communications decisions need executives in the room.
- Letting the scenario stay comfortable; inject the hard choices like encrypted backups and leaked data.
- Filing the findings without tracking them; tabletop gaps recur if nobody owns the fixes.
- Using the same scenario every year; rotate scenarios to cover new attacker tactics.
