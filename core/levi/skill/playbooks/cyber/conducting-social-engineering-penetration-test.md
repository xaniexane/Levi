## Purpose
Social engineering assessments test the human layer -- but they touch real people and carry real ethical and legal weight. This playbook governs authorized assessments: scoping, consent and legal review, scenario design, execution safeguards, and debriefing that builds awareness rather than resentment.

## When to use
- Assessing susceptibility to phishing, vishing, or physical pretexting.
- Measuring the effectiveness of security-awareness training.
- Meeting requirements for comprehensive security testing.
- After a real social-engineering incident revealed gaps.

## Prerequisites
- Written authorization from executive leadership with defined scope.
- Legal and HR review of planned scenarios, especially for in-person or call-based tests.
- Rules of engagement: prohibited tactics, data-handling, and stop conditions.
- Debrief and support plan for affected employees.

## Procedure
1. Get explicit authorization. Document scope, scenarios, and boundaries; obtain signatures from leadership, legal, and HR.
2. Define prohibited actions. Exclude tactics that cause genuine harm: threats, impersonating law enforcement, accessing personal data, or disrupting operations.
3. Design realistic scenarios. Base pretexts on current threat trends relevant to the organization; keep them believable but bounded.
4. Establish safeguards. Define how testers identify themselves if challenged, how to abort safely, and how collected data is protected and destroyed.
5. Execute in phases. Start with lower-impact scenarios (email) before higher-touch ones (calls, in-person); monitor for unintended consequences.
6. Document meticulously. Record every interaction, what was attempted, and what succeeded -- with timestamps and without unnecessary personal detail.
7. Debrief with care. Inform affected individuals promptly and privately; frame results as organizational learning, never personal failure.
8. Convert to improvement. Feed results into awareness training, verification procedures (such as call-back policies), and technical controls.

## Expected outputs
- Authorized assessment plan with legal and HR sign-off.
- Findings report focused on systemic improvements.
- Updated awareness training and verification procedures.

## Pitfalls
- Testing without full authorization exposes the organization and testers to liability.
- Public shaming of employees who fall for tests destroys trust and future reporting.
- Overly aggressive scenarios cause real distress; keep pretexts professional.
- One-off tests without follow-up training waste the learning opportunity.

## References
- NIST SP 800-115, Technical Guide to Information Security Testing and Assessment
- Social-Engineer.org framework concepts for ethical assessment
- Applicable employment and privacy law for your jurisdictions
