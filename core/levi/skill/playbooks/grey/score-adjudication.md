# Score Adjudication Process

## Purpose

Teams dispute scores — that's healthy, if the adjudication is fair. This process handles
disputes against the published rubric and the evidence, with written rulings, so scoring
ends in agreement (or documented disagreement), not resentment.

## When to use

- After scores are published, during the review window.
- Whenever a team challenges a score.

## Prerequisites

- Published scoring model and rubric.
- Evidence packages.
- Adjudication authority designated (exercise director or panel).

## Procedure

1. Open the dispute window with a deadline; collect challenges in writing with counter-
   evidence.
2. Adjudicate each dispute against the rubric and the evidence — not against opinions.
3. Issue written rulings: upheld, adjusted, or denied, with reasoning cited.
4. Adjust scores where the evidence supports it; document every change.
5. Close the window and publish final scores with the adjudication record.
6. Feed systemic disputes back into the scoring-model design for next time.

## Expected outputs

- Adjudication rulings with reasoning.
- Final scores with change log.
- Scoring-model improvement notes.

## Pitfalls

- Adjudication without the rubric — the rubric is the law.
- Open-ended dispute windows — deadline and close.
- Unwritten rulings — write them, or the dispute never ends.

## References

- Dispute-resolution literature; competition judging practices.

## Exercise role

Adjudication is where scoring earns trust — fair process, written rulings, and the
rubric as the only authority.
