# Referee Authority and Jurisdiction Design

## Purpose

Referees adjudicate the exercise in real time: was that action in bounds, does that
detection count, is the scenario still fair? This design defines their authority,
jurisdiction, and how rulings bind the teams — before the first dispute.

## When to use

- During exercise planning.
- For competitive exercises especially.

## Prerequisites

- Exercise rules and scoring model.
- Referee roster with exercise credibility.

## Procedure

1. Define referee jurisdiction: ROE interpretation, scoring disputes in real time, fairness
   calls, safety stops.
2. Define ruling authority: which rulings bind immediately, which are appealable, and to
   whom.
3. Establish the ruling process: how to call a referee, response-time expectations,
   documentation.
4. Brief all teams on referee authority before execution — no surprises mid-exercise.
5. Log every ruling with timestamp and reasoning.
6. Review rulings in the after-action for consistency.

## Expected outputs

- Referee charter with jurisdiction and authority.
- Ruling log.
- After-action consistency review.

## Pitfalls

- Undefined authority — referees nobody listens to are decorations.
- Unlogged rulings — log every one.
- Mid-exercise authority invention — charter it before, not during.

## References

- Competition-officiating literature; exercise doctrine.

## Exercise role

Referee authority is the exercise's rule of law — chartered before execution, or
disputes become arguments.
