# Commitments That Hold

## Purpose

Intentions die quietly; commitments survive because they are owned, dated,
and watched. This playbook runs the full pipeline: extract every implied
action, give each one owner and one deadline, drop what cannot get both,
and put a check-in on what remains.

## When to use

- After any meeting, call, or long thread with implied action items.
- You have decided to do something that matters and want it to survive
  your future self.
- Anywhere "I'll try" needs to become "I will, and here is how you will know."

## Steps

1. Extract every implied action from the notes — including the ones phrased
   as observations. "We should" is hiding an action. Convert it or cut it.
2. Each action gets exactly one owner: a name, never a group. Groups own
   nothing. Each action gets one deadline: a date, not "soon."
3. Anything that cannot get an owner and a deadline gets dropped —
   explicitly. The dropped list is a feature: it proves the extraction
   was honest.
4. Convert the survivors into commitments: "I will ___ by ___." Name the
   check-in — who or what checks, on what cadence. Record it somewhere you
   cannot pretend you did not: write it down, tell someone, log it.
5. The check-in happens on schedule whether or not there is progress.
   Silence is data. At the deadline: shipped, renegotiated, or released —
   each stated explicitly, never ghosted.

## Honesty notes

- Fewer, owned actions beat a long list of hopes every time.
- Renegotiating openly beats quietly slipping. The watch makes slipping
  visible, not punished.
- Lock fewer things. A locked commitment you break teaches you to stop
  locking — and then nothing is locked.
- If the commitment no longer serves, release it explicitly. Ghosting your
  own commitments is how the system rots.
