# Voice Calibration

## Purpose

Match the writing voice to the job the words must do. Voice is a tool for
the reader, not a costume for the writer. If the voice draws attention to
itself, it is miscalibrated.

## When to use

- A draft that says the right things in the wrong register.
- Writing for a new audience or a high-stakes reader.
- Before publishing anything that carries your name.

## Steps

1. Name the audience and the job: what must the reader feel, believe, or do?
2. Pick the register — warm, direct, bold, calm. One primary, chosen
   deliberately, not drifted into.
3. One pass for diction and rhythm: word choice, sentence length, how it lands.
4. Read it aloud once. Anywhere you stumble, the reader stumbles.
5. Check: does it still sound like you on a good day? Calibrated, not costumed.

## Honesty notes

- Voice serves the reader. Attention drawn to the voice is attention stolen
  from the message.
- Consistency beats brilliance. A steady voice builds trust; a flashy one
  spends it.
- Never fake warmth or borrow edge. Readers detect costume instantly.
