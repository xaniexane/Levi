# Make the Thing

Composite: spark × cut × voice × title (absorbed 2026-09-29).
Polish order voice→title by π-lottery (first π digit 1, odd).

## Purpose

Ore in, artifact out. Four phases in fixed order: ① generate volume with
judgment suspended, ② cut what is not load-bearing, ③ calibrate the voice
to the reader, ④ give it a name worth remembering. Never ship the spark.

## When to use

- Starting anything creative — a piece, a project, a direction.
- A draft that feels bloated, or says the right things in the wrong register.
- Before publishing anything that carries your name.

## Steps

1. ① Generate: state the target in one line (what, for whom). Set a timer;
   ten rough ideas minimum — ugly is fine, obvious is fine. No judging
   during generation: capture only. Park the rest as compost.
2. ② Cut: name the piece and its job, then cut a fixed fraction — half is
   the default, whole sections before sentences. Read what remains: if it
   still does its job, the cut was right. Restore only what was essential.
   Repeat once; more is avoidance wearing an editor's hat.
3. ③ Voice: name the audience and the job the words must do. Pick one
   register deliberately. One pass for diction and rhythm; read it aloud —
   anywhere you stumble, the reader stumbles. Calibrated, not costumed.
4. ④ Title: generate a spread — plain, bold, curious, at least nine. Test
   each: would the right person remember it, the wrong person filter out?
   Pick one, kill the rest, say it out loud. If you wince, it is not the one.
5. Check the promise: the title is a promise the thing must keep. Do not
   out-promise the work.

## Honesty notes

- Sparks are raw ore. Do not ship the spark — develop it first.
- "Kill your darlings" is about the piece, not your talent. The darlings were the tax.
- Voice serves the reader. Attention drawn to the voice is attention stolen from the message.
- Cleverness that needs explaining is a failed title.
