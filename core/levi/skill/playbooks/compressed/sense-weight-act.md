# Sense → Weight → Act

## Purpose

A three-beat decision pipeline where each beat belongs to a different
seat and none of them do each other's job:

- **Sense** (outward): detect demand, scout options, read the world.
  "Where is the need? What are the options?"
- **Weight** (inward): judge, weigh, teach. "Which move, and why?"
- **Act** (forward): execute with receipts. "Done — here's the proof."

## When to use

Any consequential call — pricing a service, taking a bounty, routing
money, choosing a build. One beat per seat keeps sensing honest (no
pre-judging the options it finds) and weighing honest (no inventing
evidence to reach a favorite).

## The pattern

1. **Sense first, weigh never.** The sensing pass collects options and
   demand signals and stops. It ranks nothing.
2. **Weigh on the sensed set only.** Feed the sensed options into
   triage/weigh. New options discovered mid-weigh go back to sensing —
   never get smuggled into the verdict.
3. **Act through the six gates.** Plan → Preview → Permission → Execute
   → Verify → Receipt. The verdict is an input to the gates, not a
   bypass around them.

## Honest limits

- Skipping sensing ("I already know the options") is the most common
  failure mode — it turns weighing into rationalization.
- Skipping weighing ("the data speaks for itself") hands judgment to
  whoever framed the data.
- The pipeline is slow on purpose. Speed is what the beats are
  protecting you from.
