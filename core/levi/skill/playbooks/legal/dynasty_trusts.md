# Dynasty and Perpetual Trusts

## Purpose

Explain how wealth and control pass across generations without being
taxed or fragmented at each handoff: dynasty trusts, the
generation-skipping transfer (GST) tax, trust situs strategy, and
directed-trust mechanics. This is the legal spine of the dynasty
holdings — holder and successor structures built to outlive their founder.

## The doctrine

- **Rule against perpetuities, reformed:** the common-law rule limited
  trusts to lives-in-being plus 21 years. Many states have abolished or
  extended it — Illinois permits trusts up to 360 years (765 ILCS 315/16
  as amended), effectively dynasty-capable. Situs choice determines which
  perpetuities regime governs.
- **GST tax (26 U.S.C. §§ 2601–2663):** a flat-rate tax on transfers to
  "skip persons" (grandchildren and lower generations, or trusts for
  them), *on top of* estate/gift tax. Each transferor has a GST exemption
  (unified with the lifetime exemption amount, indexed). Proper
  allocation of GST exemption to a dynasty trust on funding is the
  central planning act — missed allocation is the central malpractice
  risk.
- **Dynasty trust mechanics:** an irrevocable trust funded with
  exemption-covered assets, structured to benefit multiple generations
  without inclusion in any beneficiary's estate (spendthrift provisions,
  discretionary distributions, independent trustee elements). Assets can
  compound inside the trust envelope for the full perpetuities period.
- **Situs strategy:** states compete on trust law — no state income tax
  on accumulated trust income (e.g., Delaware, South Dakota, Nevada,
  Alaska), strong directed-trust statutes, decanting flexibility, and
  privacy. Illinois situs is viable but rarely optimal for a national
  dynasty structure; moving situs requires following the chosen state's
  nexus rules (trustee location, administration) or the benefits are
  illusory.
- **Directed trusts:** bifurcate duties — a distribution adviser or
  trust protector directs, the trustee administers. Lets the family keep
  investment and distribution control with professionals handling
  custody and accounting, with liability following the direction.
- **Grantor vs. non-grantor:** intentionally defective grantor trusts
  (IDGTs) let the grantor pay the trust's income tax (further tax-free
  wealth transfer); non-grantor trusts pay their own compressed-bracket
  tax. The choice is a modeled decision, not a default.

## Checklist

1. **Define the purpose in writing.** Who the trust serves, for how
   long, under what distribution philosophy — the statement of intent
   guides trustees for a century.
2. **Choose situs deliberately.** Compare perpetuities period, income
   taxation of trusts, directed-trust statutes, decanting, and privacy;
   establish real nexus (trustee/administration) in the chosen state.
3. **Allocate GST exemption correctly** on funding — timely Form 709,
   affirmative allocation elections; never assume automatic allocation
   covers the trust.
4. **Draft the protective provisions.** Spendthrift clause, discretionary
   (not mandatory) distributions, trust protector powers, decanting
   authority for future flexibility.
5. **Fund completely and cleanly.** Valuation at funding (appraisals for
   hard-to-value assets like business interests or IP), no retained
   powers that pull assets back into the estate (§§ 2036–2038).
6. **Administer like it matters.** Crummey notices if annual-exclusion
   gifts are used, separate accounting, trustee minutes — a dynasty
   trust neglected in administration is a lawsuit in waiting.

## Red flags

- "Set up a dynasty trust online in an afternoon" — irrevocable,
  multi-generational, tax-sensitive instruments are not template work.
- Funding without GST allocation analysis — the exemption is use-it-
  right-or-lose-it on the transfer.
- Illinois-situs default with no analysis of better trust jurisdictions.
- Retained control (grantor as sole trustee with unfettered powers)
  that triggers estate inclusion under §§ 2036/2038.
- No succession plan for the trustee itself across a 360-year horizon.
