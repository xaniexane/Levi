# LLC Operating Agreements and Veil Protection

## Purpose

Turn the LLC from a filed form into a functioning liability shield. Covers
the clauses a real operating agreement needs, transfer restrictions and
charging-order mechanics, and the maintenance habits that keep courts from
piercing the veil.

## The doctrine

Illinois does not require a written operating agreement (805 ILCS
180/15-5 permits oral or written), but an LLC without one is governed by
statutory defaults that rarely match the members' intent — including
per-capita (not per-capital) voting and distribution defaults. The
agreement is a contract among members; it should be signed, dated, and
kept with the company records.

- **Charging orders (805 ILCS 180/30-20):** a member's personal creditor
  generally gets a charging order against the member's distributional
  interest — a lien on distributions, not a right to manage the company
  or force distributions. This is the LLC's signature asset-protection
  feature, stronger than corporate share levy in most states.
- **Veil-piercing** applies to LLCs as to corporations: courts look to
  commingling, failure to observe formalities, undercapitalization, and
  use of the entity to perpetrate injustice. Illinois courts apply the
  two-prong *Sea-Land* style analysis (unity of interest + inequitable
  result). Single-member LLCs face closer scrutiny.
- **Transfer restrictions:** the agreement can restrict transfers,
  impose rights of first refusal, and define buy-sell triggers (death,
  disability, deadlock, voluntary exit) with valuation mechanics.
  Without them, a member's interest can end up owned by a stranger or an
  ex-spouse's creditor.

## Checklist

1. **Sign the agreement at formation.** Members, capital contributions
   (cash, property, services — with valuation), ownership percentages,
   voting rights.
2. **Define money mechanics.** Distribution policy and timing, tax
   distributions (enough to cover members' pass-through liability),
   capital-call procedures.
3. **Lock the exits.** Transfer restrictions, buy-sell triggers,
   valuation formula (or appraisal process), non-compete/non-solicit
   within enforceable bounds (see Illinois Freedom to Work Act limits).
4. **Assign management.** Member-managed vs. manager-managed; who can
   bind the company; banking authority.
5. **Maintain the veil.** Separate accounts, annual reports filed,
   meetings/minutes (even brief), no personal guarantees commingled,
   adequate capitalization for the business's risk profile.
6. **Review on change.** New member, new line of business, new state of
   operation — amend the agreement in writing, signed by all members.

## Red flags

- No written agreement years into operation — defaults govern, and
  nobody remembers what the defaults are.
- Verbal "we'll split everything 50/50" with undocumented unequal
  capital contributions.
- Buy-sell clause with no valuation method — a guaranteed future fight.
- Personal expenses run through the company card "temporarily."
- Charging-order protection assumed to block *all* creditors — it
  addresses members' personal creditors, not the company's own debts.
