# Circuit Breaker — The Pairing Veto

Every pairing in the expander canon is **fit-gated**: the circuit breaker
— and everything downstream of it — will NOT pair things that do not go
together. A refused pair is a verdict, not a failure.

## The move

1. **Name the pair and the material.** What two are you trying to mix,
   and what are you mixing them into?
2. **Check the fit.** Do the twins have a real seam — a place where one's
   output is the other's input? Or are you forcing it because the pair
   worked elsewhere?
3. **Veto honestly.** If the fit is not there, say so: "no pair." The
   breaker trips, the run stops cleanly, and you walk away with the
   material intact — not a Frankenstein.

## Pairing law

- A forced pair is worse than no pair: it spends real effort to produce
  staged mixing, which is dishonest by definition.
- The veto is cheap; the damage of a bad mix is not.
- "No pair" is a complete answer. Move on to the next combination.

## When to reach for it

Before ANY twin-pair run. It is the gate every other playbook in this
batch walks through. Advisory only; nothing here acts, sends, or spends.
