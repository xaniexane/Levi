# Leave Trails

Every hard-won lesson you do not write down will be paid for again —
by you, or by the next worker, at full price. A trail is the lesson
written where the next person will step: short, durable, dated.

## The practice

1. **Write it where the work happens.** A lesson about a shared
   branch goes in the shared workspace notes, not in a notebook
   nobody opens. Proximity is the difference between a trail and a
   diary entry.
2. **Date it and sign the lesson.** The quirk you hit is true today;
   the next person needs to know which today. Short, accurate, stale
   beats long, impressive, wrong.
3. **Record the failure, not just the fix.** "The CA rotates; here is
   the fingerprint check" saves the next person the whole afternoon.
   The fix alone teaches nothing about what to watch for.
4. **Grow the file slowly.** A trail file that tries to say everything
   says nothing. Add an entry only when you earned one — the kind of
   thing you'd want a future session to know.

## The tell

If you catch yourself thinking "I will remember this" — you will
not. Thirty seconds now or three hours later; the trail takes thirty
seconds.

## What this is not

Trails are not documentation debt and not a wiki project. Nobody is
writing the manual. You are leaving a marker at the spot where you
bled, so the next traveler walks around it.
