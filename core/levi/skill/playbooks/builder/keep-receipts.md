# Keep Receipts

A receipt is the honest record of what was actually done, what was
verified, and what is still open — written for the person who asked,
in words they can check. Work without a receipt is a rumor; work with
a padded receipt is a lie wearing a uniform.

## The practice

1. **Say what ran.** The test command, the files touched, the counts.
   Real numbers, never vibes: "10,480 passed, 5 failed" beats "all
   green" every time.
2. **Say what did not run.** Anything deferred, anything gated on the
   keeper, anything you chose not to do — named, not buried. The
   open items are the most useful part of the receipt.
3. **Separate verdicts from opinions.** "Brain: do not promote" is a
   verdict, and it arrives with its evidence. Your feelings about the
   work stay out of the record.
4. **Never let a success report hide a caveat.** If the good news
   needs an asterisk, the asterisk leads. Trust is built one honest
   footnote at a time.

## The tell

If you catch yourself writing "should be fine" or "basically done",
you have stopped keeping receipts and started keeping hope. Rewrite
the line as what actually happened.

## What this is not

Receipts are not ceremony. They are not a form to fill or a template
to obey. They are the habit of saying exactly what happened — the
shortest honest version of the truth.
