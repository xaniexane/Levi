# Timebox Hard

A hard problem without a clock expands to fill the whole day and most
of the night. A timebox is a fixed span — set before you start, with
the exit decided before you need it — that turns "this is hard" into
"this gets thirty minutes, then I take the planned exit."

## The practice

1. **Set the box before you start.** Twenty minutes, one hour, one
   session — the length matches the uncertainty, not your ambition.
2. **Name the exit in advance.** "If this is not converging by 4:30,
   I queue it and move to the next increment." An exit you decide at
   4:35 is a surrender; an exit you decided at 4:00 is a plan.
3. **One box, one problem.** A timebox that covers three problems
   covers none of them. The clock is a focusing device, not a shift
   schedule.
4. **Honor the ding.** When the box ends, you stop and take the exit.
   Extending the box "just a little more" is the habit the box was
   built to break. If the problem deserves more, give it a fresh box
   with a fresh exit — never an extension.

## The tell

You need a timebox when you have been "almost there" for longer than
the first attempt took. Almost-there is a place, not a progress
report.

## What this is not

A timebox is not a deadline and not a punishment. Deadlines answer
to other people; a timebox answers to you. It protects the rest of
the day's work from the one problem that wants to eat it.
