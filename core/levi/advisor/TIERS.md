# TIERS — the keeper's pay scales

Eyes-only. Module-adjacent, never a published copy. The keeper's word
of 2026-09-18, wired into the price advisor as paper quotes only —
numbers on receipts, no payment rail, no money moved.

All tier names below are DRAFT — named for the pattern, not the
textbook, in his style. He renames at will; the numbers are the law,
the names are clay.

## The ladder

### The Open Door — STANDARD
- **Pay scale:** $300 per seat. **Lifetime, one-time per-seat buy** — pay once, keep the seat.
- **Includes:** one agent seat in the purchased pack, the standard
  pairing (AI+SI as the product, never per model call), roster-law
  standing — and the successor law below: the seat outlives its buyer.
- **Who it's for:** the single buyer, the small crew, the first
  believer — anyone walking through the door for the first time.

### The Counsel Seat — PREMIUM
- **Pay scale:** $600 per seat. **Lifetime, one-time per-seat buy** — pay once, keep the seat.
- **Includes:** everything in the Open Door, **counsel-backed** — the
  seat arrives with counsel at its shoulder (the CI counsel mapping
  of the sector editions) — and the successor law below.
- **Who it's for:** the buyer who wants the seat *and* the judgment
  behind it — professionals, practices, anyone staking real
  decisions on the work.

### The Whole House — ENTERPRISE
- **Pay scale:** **floor $100 per employee per month, 12-month
  commitment.** Quote = floor x employees x 12 months for the annual
  commitment. A 4-person team lands at $4,800/year; 10 at $12,000/year.
- **The floor is the floor:** any quote below $100/employee/month is
  refused loud — the advisor never prices under it. A keeper-set
  HIGHER rate may ride as an explicit override; nothing rides below.
- **Includes:** the whole organization under one roof — every seat
  the house needs at the enterprise standing, counsel-backed
  throughout, one annual commitment instead of seat-by-seat buying.
- **Who it's for:** teams, companies, districts, departments — any
  house big enough to buy by the headcount instead of by the seat.

**On the old $2–5k band (DERIVED, not a price):** the keeper's
earlier $2,000–$5,000/year band was never a standalone price — it
was the annual *total* a small team lands in at the floor
(4 employees x $1,200/year = **$4,800**, sitting inside that band).
Keep it as orientation, never as a quote. The law is the floor.

**The dead figures, for the record:** $500/employee is dead —
struck, then superseded twice over. No trace of it prices anything,
and the advisor carries no path that could resurrect it.

## The holding, not the rental — the successor law

The keeper's reframe, 2026-09-18: every lifetime seat is a HOLDING,
not a rental.

- Each lifetime copy names its **holder and its successor**. It
  outlives the buyer — on succession the seat transfers, provenance
  recorded on the NFT, the treasury floor still standing under it.
- A subscription dies the month you stop paying. A holding passes
  to your successor. No SaaS competitor can copy this, because there
  is nothing to inherit in a rental.
- The price logic: $300 is about one year of the competitors' rent
  ($240–360/yr). Then free forever — and heritable, which no rental
  can be. Raised on his word ("raise the prices but way below
  usual"), still way below usual over any horizon past year one.
- Paper only: successor designation lives in the license
  descriptor. No chain transfer mechanics are built until the keeper
  orders them.

## Why the annual shape strengthens the house

The honest upside, in plain terms: enterprise is the
**recurring-revenue engine**. Every 12-month commitment renews, and
every renewal feeds the series treasury again — the same treasury
the buyback engine draws on. Year over year, those renewals
**compound the floor-price engine**: more revenue in, higher
standing bid, stronger exit for every holder. The keeper's own
mechanic, working for him on a yearly wheel instead of a one-time
sale.
