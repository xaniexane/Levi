✏️ Unit 6 Essay Activity

Standards
Topics: 7.1 Contextualizing Period 7, 7.4 The Progressives, 7.9 Great Depression, 7.10 New Deal, 7.15 Comparison in Period 7
Learning Objectives: Unit 7A Explain the context in which America grew into its role as a world power, Unit 7D Compare the goals and effects of the Progressive reform movement, Unit 7J Explain the causes of the Great Depression and its effects on the economy, Unit 7K Explain how the Great Depression and the New Deal impacted American political, social, and economic life over time, Unit 7O Compare the relative significance of the major events of the first half of the 20th century in shaping American identity
Historical Developments: 7.1.I.C, 7.1.II.B, 7.1.III.A, 7.1.III.C, 7.3.I, 7.3.II, 7.3.III
Historical Thinking Skills: HTS-1 Developments and Processes, HTS-3 Source Claims and Evidence, HTS-4 Contextualization, HTS-5 Making Connections, HTS-6 Argumentation
Reasoning Processes: RP-1 Comparison, RP-2 Causation
Objectives:

Students will be able to construct an AP-Style Long Essay using resources from Unit 6 of Life, Liberty, and the Pursuit of Happiness to practice constructing a historical argument.

Students will be able to analyze the AP Long Essay Rubric to reflect on their own skills in the context of the AP Exam.
Resources:
Handout A: Long Essay Rubric
Handout B: Long Essay Options
Handout C: Writing Reflection

I. Warm-Up

Students review Handout A: Long Essay Rubric.

Remind students this rubric is used for scoring all Long Essay Questions on the exam and they should craft their response with these criteria in mind.

Address any student questions on rubric categories.

II. Exploration

Have students read Handout B: Long Essay Options for the essay question and directions.

Clarify any questions on what the prompt is asking.

III. Application

Allow students 5 to 10 minutes to brainstorm their responses to their selected prompt.

After brainstorming time has passed, announce that students should now begin writing their essay. It is suggested you time students at this point in the year so they become used to writing for a deadline. It is suggested that students use 35 minutes to plan and write their essay.

IV. Conclusion

After the teacher has had the opportunity to evaluate students’ essays on the basis of the rubric, students should be provided the opportunity to reflect on their writing progress and set intentional goals for growth, using Handout C: Writing Reflection.
