✏️ Unit 7 Essay Activity

Standards
Topics: 8.3 The Red Scare, 8.6 Early Steps in the Civil Rights Movement (1940s and 1950s), 8.9 Great Society, 8.10 The African American Civil Rights Movement (1960s), 8.14 Society in Transition
Themes: NAT American and National Identity, PCE Politics and Power, SOC Social Structures
Learning Objectives: Unit 8C Explain the causes and effects of the Red Scare after World War II, Unit 8G Explain how and why the civil rights movements developed and expanded from 1945 to 1960, Unit 8J Explain the causes and effects of continuing policy debates about the role of the federal government over time, Unit 8L Explain how and why various groups responded to calls for the expansion of civil rights from 1960 to 1980
Historical Developments: 8.1.II.A, 8.2.I, 8.2.I.A, 8.2.I.B.i, 8.2.I.C, 8.2.II.C, 8.2.III.A, 8.2.III.B.i, 8.2.III.C, 8.2.III.E, 8.2.III.F
Historical Thinking Skills: HTS-1 Developments and Processes, HTS-2 Sourcing and Situation, HTS-3 Source Claims and Evidence, HTS-4 Contextualization, HTS-5 Making Connections, HTS-6 Argumentation
Reasoning Processes: RP-1 Comparison, RP-2 Causation, RP-3 Continuity and Change
Objectives:

Students will be able to construct an AP-Style Long Essay using resources from Unit 7 of Life, Liberty, and the Pursuit of Happiness to practice constructing a historical argument.

Students will be able to analyze the AP Long Essay Rubric to reflect on their own skills in the context of the AP Exam.
Resources:
Handout A: Long Essay Rubric
Handout B: Long Essay Options
Handout C: Writing Reflection

I. Warm-Up

Students review Handout A: Long Essay Rubric.

Remind students this rubric is used for scoring all Long Essay Questions on the exam and they should craft their response with these criteria in mind.

Address any student questions on rubric categories.

II. Exploration

Have students read Handout B: Long Essay Options for the essay question and directions.

Clarify any questions on what the prompt is asking.

III. Application

Allow students 5 to 10 minutes to brainstorm their responses to their selected prompt.

After brainstorming time has passed, announce that students should now begin writing their essay. It is suggested you time students at this point in the year so they become used to writing for a deadline. It is suggested that students use 35 minutes to plan and write their essay.

IV. Conclusion

After the teacher has had the opportunity to evaluate students’ essays on the basis of the rubric, students should be provided the opportunity to reflect on their writing progress and set intentional goals for growth, using Handout C: Writing Reflection.
