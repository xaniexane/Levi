# The Killed Third Places — evening hunt 2026-09-20

Theme: (e) GAMES — the social lobbies and kid-safe worlds the giants built,
ran as third places (not storefronts, not feeds: *places*), and then killed
when they stopped feeding the quarterly numbers. The pattern to turn against
its makers: the third place is load-bearing social infrastructure that a
profit calendar cannot keep alive. The addition is one that needs no profit
calendar at all.

Morning's run (daily-20260920-attention) covered the attention/community
bargain in giant-patterns terms (TikTok FYP, Discord hostage graph, Copilot
training toll). This vein is distinct: not the extraction trade, the
*starvation* trade — communities killed by neglect, acquisition pivots, and
monetization refocus rather than by malice.

## The four finds

### 1. PlayStation Home (Sony, 2008–2015) — the evicted tenant class
A persistent 3D social world on PS3: avatars, private spaces you decorated
with trophies, public minigames (bowling alley, arcade cabinets), branded
event spaces (Ghostbusters firehouse meetups, E3 booth). 19M users by 2011,
average session 70 minutes — genuinely used, genuinely loved by a niche.
Sony shut it down March 31, 2015 citing a "shifting landscape"; refused a PS4
port; published no new content after Nov 12, 2014; no refunds on purchased
virtual goods (TechTudo reports no reimbursement). Players lost bought content
with no recourse. The kill wasn't malice — it was strategic drift: the PS4
generation was "focused on games", so the third place had no org chart to
live in. Load-bearing lesson: server-side third places die when the
platform's strategy changes; purchased virtual goods are rent paid to a
landlord who can evict you.

### 2. Miiverse (Nintendo, 2012–2017) — positivity by design, killed by drift
A social network built *into* games: game-tied community pages, handwritten
posts, and heavy human moderation (posts were delayed in a review queue —
the famous "please wait, your post is being checked" — which made it the only
major network that was wholesome by construction, not by algorithm).
Nintendo ended it Nov 7, 2017 because "many users are shifting to social
networking services" — the third place lost to the feed. 112 titles lost
in-game features (Mario Maker comments, Smash/Mario Kart 8 replay sharing,
Splatoon plaza posts). Players could download their own posts but not
comments — the *graph* stayed Nintendo's, or rather, died with Nintendo's
servers. Load-bearing lesson: human-moderated, slow-by-design social space
was a real product ("the only wholesome network"), killed because it couldn't
compete on engagement velocity with Facebook/Twitter. The honest inversion:
small + slow + local doesn't need to compete.

### 3. Club Penguin (Disney/New Horizon, 2005–2017) — moderation as product
The canonical kid-safe third place: minigames, parties, puffles, and —
critically — a moderation operation that made it a haven parents trusted.
Disney bought it (2007, $350M-ish deal), then killed the browser original
March 30, 2017 to force-migrate players to the mobile reboot Club Penguin
Island. Island introduced in-app purchases and advertising — "perceived as a
betrayal of the original game's principles" — couldn't carry progress/items
over, alienated the core, and was itself killed Dec 20, 2018 with the whole
Kelowna team laid off (three weeks after being greenlit for two more years;
Disney HR cited "global competition"). The fan remakes that survive are
noted for lacking the moderation that made the original a haven. Load-bearing
lesson: the safe third place was a *moderation service with a game attached*;
the reboot monetized the game and dropped the service. The pattern to invert:
third places don't die from lack of players — they die from monetization
refocus.

### 4. AltspaceVR (Microsoft, acquired 2017, killed 2023) — the saved-then-harvested
Launched May 2015; ran out of money summer 2017 and announced shutdown;
Microsoft swooped in two months later and bought it. Six years later —
January 2023, amid 10,000 layoffs — Microsoft killed it on March 10, 2023,
citing a pivot to Mesh "workplace experiences". The entire AltspaceVR team
cut; consumer social VR sacrificed to the enterprise calendar. This is the
sly-generosity trade in its purest form: the "rescue" was a six-year
talent-and-tech harvest; the community was never the asset. Load-bearing
lesson: a third place owned by a giant is on a clock that starts at
acquisition. The community is inventory.

## The pattern (for the archive)
Third places die four ways: strategic drift (Home), engagement-velocity loss
(Miiverse), monetization refocus (Club Penguin), acquisition pivot
(AltspaceVR). All four share the root: the third place's cost is constant
(moderation, servers, care) while its revenue is indirect, so any owner with
a quarterly calendar eventually starves it. The inversion LEVI ships: a third
place whose cost is ~zero — no servers, no moderation staff, no monetization
— because it is local-first, owner-operated, and portable. It cannot be
starved because it is never fed. That is `core/levi/games/parlor.py`: the
parlor — handles, a bulletin board, and game nights for LEVI's warehouse
games, stored in `~/.levi/games/parlor/` as owner-only JSON. The Miiverse
inversion: no review queue, no algorithm — small and local is the moderation.
The Club Penguin inversion: the safety comes from being *your* household's
place, not a corporation's service. The Home inversion: nothing to purchase,
nothing to revoke. The AltspaceVR inversion: nobody can acquire the parlor
because there's nothing to acquire.
