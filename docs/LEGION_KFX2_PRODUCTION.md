## (a) Competitor teardown — marketplaces + one creature-IP reference

Sources: public secondary reporting only (marked below). Everything marked
with (verify) could not be confirmed from a primary source in this pass.

### OpenSea

- Mechanics: general-purpose NFT marketplace, multi-chain (Ethereum,
  Polygon, Solana, Arbitrum, Optimism, Avalanche, BNB Chain and more —
  review sources say 19+ networks; exact current count (verify)).
- Fee structure as stated: standard marketplace fee 2.5% on sales (seller
  side). OpenSea Pro offered 0%–0.5% for a limited time (verify — dates
  and current status not reconfirmed). No buyer fee.
- Royalties: originally creator-set, commonly 5–10%. During the Blur
  fee war, OpenSea moved creator earnings to optional with a stated
  minimum of 0.5% unless on-chain enforcement was present; their own
  statement at the time cited on-chain data showing ~80% of sales paying
  no creator royalties. (Sources: CoinMarketCap, TheNewsCrypto, Bulb —
  these describe a 2023-era event; the current 2026 royalty regime was
  not re-verified — verify.)
- What creators/collectors love: largest user base and selection, broad
  chain support, creator tooling, trusted brand, mobile apps.
- What they hate: royalty erosion (the 5–10% → optional 0.5% arc is the
  textbook case), fee wars that punish creators for liquidity, gas costs
  on L1, speculation-first culture.
- Strategic read: OpenSea's royalty collapse is the case study in what
  happens when the marketplace's revenue depends on volume, not on
  creator success. When Blur pulled volume, creator earnings were the
  first thing traded away.

### Blur

- Mechanics: Ethereum-only NFT marketplace built for professional traders:
  data-heavy interface, multi-marketplace aggregation, fast trading.
- Fee structure as stated: 0% platform fee; enforces a minimum 0.5%
  creator royalty (as reported; exact current enforcement mechanics
  (verify)).
- Distribution model: BLUR token rewards to traders — the marketplace
  subsidized volume with its own token. This is the documented engine of
  its rise (Dune Analytics volume/trader data cited in contemporary
  reporting).
- What traders love: zero fees, speed, aggregation, rewards for trading
  itself.
- What creators/collectors hate: royalty floors that eroded creator
  income industry-wide; reward structures widely criticized for
  incentivizing wash trading and flip-driven volume (verify — this is
  the widely repeated critique; we did not independently verify
  wash-trade volumes).
- Strategic read: Blur is the marketplace-as-casino perfected — the venue
  optimizes for velocity, and the collectible is a chip. Everything they
  monetize depends on the collectible being separable from any meaning
  beyond price.

### Wash trading and royalty erosion — the industry's twin weaknesses

- Wash trading: self-dealing to fake volume/prices, incentivized by
  reward programs that pay traders for volume regardless of intent. (Wide
  public reporting; figures vary and were not independently verified.)
- Royalty erosion: marketplaces competing for volume drove creator
  royalties from 5–10% toward optional 0.5% or zero. OpenSea's own 2023
  statement (cited above) put ~80% of sales paying no creator royalties.
- Both are the same failure: the venue's incentives (volume) overpowered
  the creator's (sustained value). The collectible became a chip, the
  creator became a supplier.

### Creature-IP positioning reference: Pudgy Penguins

Why this reference: the most publicly documented creature-IP playbook of
the modern era — character universe first, then brand expansion through
licensing. (Figures below from secondary reporting; flagged where
unverified.)

- Launch: 8,888 NFTs on Ethereum, August 2021.
- Turnaround: entrepreneur Luca Netz acquired the project April 2022 for
  750 ETH (~$2.5M at the time, per secondary reporting), becoming CEO.
- Positioning mechanics (publicly documented): the NFT drop was the
  attention event, not the business. The business was built through
  physical licensing — Pudgy Toys in Walmart, Target, and Amazon — with
  the retail operation (Igloo Inc.) kept legally and financially separate
  from the token. Later: PENGU token on Solana (Dec 17, 2024, 88.89B max
  supply per secondary reporting), airdropped to eligible NFT holders
  and toy owners; continuing character licensing deals (e.g. a 2026
  co-branded companion-robot license).
- What the playbook teaches: (1) characters before commerce — the IP was
  legible and beloved before anything was sold beyond the drop;
  (2) physical retail as the trust engine — a toy on a Walmart shelf is
  credibility a token can't buy; (3) separation of concerns — the toy
  business and the token economy were kept distinct, which protected
  both; (4) licensing as expansion — the IP compounds by letting others
  build with it under contract.
- What the playbook does NOT teach (its weaknesses): the drop was still
  a speculation-first event; holders endured a long credibility gap
  before the turnaround; licensing is a slow, legal, trust-heavy
  business, not a mint-day event.

### Negative reference: the rigged-drop failure mode

- MekaVerse (public reporting, 2021): the highly anticipated drop was
  publicly accused of skewing rarity distribution toward the team and
  insiders instead of the promised random raffle. Regardless of the
  final truth, the damage was to trust, not price — the community's
  accusation became the story.
- Lesson: distribution fairness is not a technical detail, it IS the
  launch. Any structure where the team can plausibly be accused of
  self-dealing at mint inherits this failure mode automatically.

## (b) KFX² — absorb, reverse, amplify, return unreplicable

KFX² runs on strengths AND weaknesses (2026-09-21 correction). Their
strengths get amplified into our unreplicable signature, not just their
weaknesses reversed.

### ABSORB — their patterns worth taking

1. **OpenSea's discovery surface.** The broad, searchable marketplace is
   a genuine strength: collectors need somewhere to browse. Absorbed as:
   the universe needs a public legible surface — but OURS is a
   story-first surface (entities shown with their provenance and
   continuity), not a price-first grid.
2. **Blur's trader tooling, stripped of the casino.** Fast, data-rich,
   aggregator-grade interfaces are real craft. Absorbed as: whatever we
   build for collectors gets the same engineering seriousness — but
   pointed at provenance and story, not at velocity.
3. **Pudgy Penguins' characters-before-commerce.** The IP is legible and
   beloved before anything is sold. Absorbed as: Legion entities exist in
   the universe (with continuity inside LEVI's physics) before any
   collectible event is ever discussed. The story is the asset; the
   collectible is the receipt.
4. **Pudgy Penguins' licensing separation.** Toy business and token
   economy kept distinct. Absorbed as: if Legion ever has multiple
   economic surfaces (collectibles, licensing, media), they are kept
   contractually and structurally separate — no shared failure surface.

### REVERSE — their weaknesses, flipped

| Their failure | Our reversal |
|---|---|
| Wash trading / reward-for-volume | No volume rewards exist in our architecture. The collectible's value is its provenance — a provenance you can verify can't be faked by self-dealing, because the story is in the ledger, not the chart. |
| Royalty erosion (5–10% → optional 0.5%) | We never enter the venue game. Legion economics are defined by us, stated plainly, and not subject to a marketplace's fee war — because the venue's incentives never touch the creator's terms. |
| Speculation-first drops | No drop mechanics in this plan at all. If a collectible event ever exists, it comes AFTER the universe is legible — the Pudgy pattern, not the MekaVerse pattern. |
| Rigged-distribution accusations | Distribution, if any ever occurs, is designed for fairness first and published before the fact. The team-allocation question is answered in public before anyone asks. |
| Tradable-JPEG severability (Blur's chip) | The core reversal: Legion entities are inseparable from their story. You can't separate the collectible from its provenance because the provenance is the product. |
| Venue-owned economics (OpenSea fee wars) | Creator terms are set by the universe's law, not negotiated downward by whichever marketplace has volume this quarter. |

### AMPLIFY — their strengths, made unreplicable

1. **Marketplace-as-casino → universe-as-lineage.** They optimized for
   velocity; we optimize for continuity. A Legion entity accumulates
   provenance inside LEVI's physics — its history is structural, not
   asserted. UNREPLICABLE: their entire cost structure (fees, rewards,
   aggregation) exists to trade chips fast. Ours doesn't trade chips —
   there is nothing to wash, no volume to subsidize, no casino to run.
   They cannot build lineage without destroying how they eat.
2. **OpenSea's discovery scale → story-first discovery.** They show you
   the market; we show you the universe. UNREPLICABLE: their discovery
   is price-ranked because price is all their items have. Our entities
   carry story, so discovery is narrative — a surface only a universe
   can build.
3. **Pudgy's characters-before-commerce → continuity-before-commerce.**
   They made characters legible before selling; we make entities
   CONTINUOUS before selling — the entity has a lived history in the
   system before any collectible event. UNREPLICABLE: their continuity
   is marketing; ours is physics (local-first ledger, deterministic
   record). They can claim story; we can show the ledger.
4. **Blur's trader-grade craft → collector-grade craft.** Same engineering
   seriousness, aimed at provenance instead of velocity: rarity that is
   real (derived from actual history), records that are verifiable,
   terms that are stated. UNREPLICABLE: trader craft serves speculation;
   ours serves verification — different physics, different product.
5. **Licensing separation → structural separation as law.** If Legion
   ever touches multiple economic surfaces, separation is architectural
   from day one, not repaired later like Pudgy's (which was built
   post-crisis). UNREPLICABLE: retrofitting trust is expensive; building
   it in is ours.

### The signature, in one line

They sell the chip; we grow the lineage. The collectible that can't be
separated from its story is unreplicable by any venue whose business is
