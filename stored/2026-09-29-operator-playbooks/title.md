# Name the Thing

## Purpose

Give the thing a name worth remembering. A title is a promise and a filter:
it should attract the right people and let the wrong ones self-select out.
A title that pleases everyone reaches no one.

## When to use

- Naming anything: projects, pieces, products, talks, lists.
- A working title that embarrasses you.
- Before publishing — the title is the first impression, and often the only one.

## Steps

1. State what the thing is and who it is for, each in one line.
2. Generate a spread: plain options, bold options, curious options. At least nine.
3. Test each: would the right person click, open, or remember this? Would the
   wrong person filter themselves out?
4. Pick one. Kill the rest — a shortlist kept "just in case" is indecision
   with a nicer name.
5. Say it out loud. If you wince, it is not the one.

## Honesty notes

- Cleverness that needs explaining is a failed title.
- The title is a promise the thing must keep. Do not out-promise the work.
- Take the stand. The specific title beats the safe one every time.
