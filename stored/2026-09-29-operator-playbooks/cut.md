# Cut Ruthlessly

## Purpose

Improve by removing. Whatever survives the cut was load-bearing; whatever
did not was weight. Cutting hurts because of sunk cost, not because the
material was good.

## When to use

- A draft, plan, or list that feels bloated.
- After generating raw material, to refine it.
- When something is "fine" but not sharp.

## Steps

1. Name the piece and its job: what must it do, and for whom?
2. Cut a fixed fraction — half is the default. Cut whole sections before
   trimming sentences.
3. Read what remains. Does it still do its job? If yes, the cut was right.
4. If something essential died, restore only that — not its neighbors.
5. Repeat once. Two passes is usually enough; more is avoidance wearing an
   editor's hat.

## Honesty notes

- "Kill your darlings" is about the piece, not your talent. The darlings
  were the tax.
- What remains after an honest cut is always stronger. Trust the remains.
- Cutting is not the same as shrinking timidly. Commit to the fraction, then cut.
