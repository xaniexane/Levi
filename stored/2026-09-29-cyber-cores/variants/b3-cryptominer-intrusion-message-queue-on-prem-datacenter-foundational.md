# Cryptominer Intrusion Defense for Message Queues in On-Premises Datacenter (Foundational Maturity)

## Purpose

Unauthorized miners burn CPU/GPU and cloud spend, and — more importantly — prove an attacker has code execution on your assets. Treat every miner as a foothold to investigate, not just a performance problem. Applies to Message Queues in On-Premises Datacenter at Foundational maturity.

## When to use

- Sustained high CPU/GPU on hosts or cloud instances with no business workload, especially at night/weekends, plus connections to known mining pools on Stratum ports.
- Cloud billing spikes for compute in regions you do not use, or new instances launched by unfamiliar principals.
- EDR detections for xmrig or similar binaries, scheduled tasks re-fetching miner payloads, or WMI persistence entries.
- Lens: Best telemetry: queue depth/lag metrics + producer/consumer auth logs + dead-letter queues. Full network tap/SPAN coverage at core and distribution layers feeding IDS and full-packet capture.

## Prerequisites

- Written authorization from the asset owner and incident commander before active containment.
- Access to Centralized syslog from all infrastructure with NTP-synced timestamps for cross-device correlation.

## Procedure

1. **Authorize and scope.** Open a ticket, confirm written authorization, and preserve evidence before changing anything.
2. **Detect and collect.** Hunt the signals above in Queue metrics: depth spikes, consumer lag, and poison-message dead-letter queues and Authentication logs for producers/consumers; new queue/topic creation events; run `rabbitmqctl list_queues name messages consumers 2>/dev/null | head -20` then `kafka-consumer-groups --bootstrap-server <broker> --describe --all-groups 2>/dev/null | head -20`.
3. **Analyze.** Trace the miner's persistence and delivery: the initial access vector (exposed service, weak SSH, vulnerable app) matters more than the miner itself. Then: Check for dual-use: many miner campaigns also exfiltrate credentials or deploy additional payloads — sweep for those.
4. **Contain and harden.** Close the entry vector (patch the exploited service, enforce key-based SSH, remove internet exposure), then block mining pool domains at DNS. Asset layer: Authentication on all clients, TLS in transit, authorization per queue/topic, quotas on producers. Environment layer: Physical access controls with badge logs correlatable to logical access during investigations.
5. **Document and improve.** Define one escalation trigger in advance (e.g., confirmed credential theft, more than 3 hosts) that moves the case from analyst to incident commander. Record the timeline, indicators, actions taken, and lessons learned.

## Expected outputs

- Scoped timeline; affected assets, accounts, and data with containment status each.
- Hardening verified effective; detection improvements filed.

## Pitfalls

- Killing the miner without removing persistence — it returns on reboot or on schedule.
- Writing it off as 'just a miner' without investigating the initial access that delivered it.
