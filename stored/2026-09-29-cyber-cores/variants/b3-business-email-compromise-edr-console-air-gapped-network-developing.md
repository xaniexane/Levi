# Business Email Compromise Defense for EDR Consoles in Air-Gapped Networks (Developing Maturity)

## Purpose

Attackers operate inside a compromised mailbox (or a lookalike domain) to redirect payments, harvest invoices, or pivot to executives. Dwell time is often weeks; the fraud, not the malware, is the payload. Applies to EDR Consoles in Air-Gapped Networks at Developing maturity.

## When to use

- Mailbox audit logs show inbox rules created to move or delete messages containing 'invoice', 'payment', 'wire', or executive names — the classic BEC rule footprint.
- Outbound messages to finance vendors from a slightly altered domain, or a legitimate mailbox suddenly sending payment-change requests with urgent tone.
- Logons to the mailbox from foreign IPs or anonymizing VPNs followed by long read-only sessions (the attacker learning the payment workflow before striking).
- Lens: Best telemetry: raw process/network telemetry + detection history + sensor health inventory. Local SIEM/log collector inside the enclave (no cloud shipping); removable-media transfer logs.

## Prerequisites

- Written authorization from the asset owner and incident commander before active containment.
- Access to Strict allowlisting logs: every binary execution recorded, since the set is small and known.

## Procedure

1. **Authorize and scope.** Open a ticket, confirm written authorization, and preserve evidence before changing anything.
2. **Detect and collect.** Hunt the signals above in Full process trees with command lines, hashes, and network connections for suspect hosts and Detection and response action history (isolations, kills, quarantines); run `# conceptually (EDR APIs differ):` then `edr-cli hunt process --hostname <host> --since 24h --tree`.
3. **Analyze.** Export all inbox/transport rules for the affected mailbox and diff against a known-good baseline; BEC rules often hide with names like ';' or whitespace. Then: Trace every payment instruction the mailbox sent in the dwell window: which were acted on, which bank details changed, and whether funds are recallable.
4. **Contain and harden.** Require out-of-band (voice/video) verification for any payment detail change, no exceptions, and alert on new inbox rules fleet-wide. Asset layer: Tamper protection locked with console-only disable, auto-isolation policies for high-severity detections. Environment layer: Data diodes or strictly controlled one-way transfers; media sanitization procedures for anything crossing.
5. **Document and improve.** Assign RACI for each procedure step (who detects, who contains, who communicates) and rehearse it quarterly. Record the timeline, indicators, actions taken, and lessons learned.

## Expected outputs

- Scoped timeline; affected assets, accounts, and data with containment status each.
- Hardening verified effective; detection improvements filed.

## Pitfalls

- Focusing only on the compromised mailbox while the attacker already harvested the vendor's thread and will re-target from another account.
- Treating a lookalike-domain BEC as an IT problem alone — finance process change is the actual fix.
