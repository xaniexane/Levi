# Password Spraying Defense for Microsoft Entra ID Tenants in Multi-Cloud (Managed Maturity)

## Purpose

Attackers try a small set of common passwords against many usernames, staying under per-account lockout thresholds. It is quieter than stuffing and specifically tuned to evade lockout policies, often running for days or weeks. Applies to Microsoft Entra ID Tenants in Multi-Cloud at Managed maturity.

## When to use

- A handful of failed logons per account (1-3) spread across hundreds of accounts from one or a few IPs, repeating on a schedule — the classic low-and-slow pattern.
- The same few passwords attempted everywhere (SeasonYear!, Company123, Password1); check by aggregating failed-logon password hashes where your IdP logs them.
- Spray bursts correlated with newly published password lists or timed to holidays/weekends when response is slower.
- Lens: Best telemetry: Entra sign-in + audit logs streamed to Log Analytics/SIEM with 30+ day retention. Cloud-agnostic SIEM normalizing AWS CloudTrail, Azure Activity Log, and GCP Audit Logs into one schema.

## Prerequisites

- Written authorization from the asset owner and incident commander before active containment.
- Access to CSPM coverage across all clouds with consistent misconfiguration alerting.

## Procedure

1. **Authorize and scope.** Open a ticket, confirm written authorization, and preserve evidence before changing anything.
2. **Detect and collect.** Hunt the signals above in Sign-in logs (interactive, non-interactive, service principal) with risk detections and MFA details and Audit logs: app registrations, consent grants, Conditional Access changes, role assignments; run `# Microsoft Graph (delegated admin): recent risky sign-ins` then `Get-MgAuditLogSignIn -Filter "riskState eq 'atRisk'" -Top 25 | Select-Object CreatedDateTime, UserPrincipalName, IpAddress, @{n='Risk';e={$_.RiskDetail}}`.
3. **Analyze.** Reconstruct the spray window: first-seen to last-seen per source IP, and list every account with even one success inside the window — those are confirmed or suspected takeovers. Then: Check whether sprayed accounts share traits (same OU, same onboarding batch, service accounts) to find how the username list was built.
4. **Contain and harden.** Smart lockout (soft lockout with increasing delays) plus alerting on distributed low-volume failures, not just lockouts. Asset layer: Conditional Access: require phishing-resistant MFA, compliant devices, block legacy auth and risky sign-ins. Environment layer: Federated identity across clouds from one IdP; consistent tagging and logging standards per cloud.
5. **Document and improve.** Pre-authorize low-risk containment (host isolation, session revoke, password reset) in the SOAR playbook with full audit logging. Record the timeline, indicators, actions taken, and lessons learned.

## Expected outputs

- Scoped timeline; affected assets, accounts, and data with containment status each.
- Hardening verified effective; detection improvements filed.

## Pitfalls

- Per-account lockout thresholds alone will not catch spraying; you need cross-account velocity detection.
- Treating one success as an isolated incident misses the campaign — always scope the full spray window.
